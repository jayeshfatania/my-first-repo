## 1. Ticks

**Key:** `ticks`
**Trigger:** Month 3-10 (March to October)
**Terrain:** Woodland, heathland, long grass, moorland

**Title:** Tick check season

**Alert body:**
Tick season is underway. After walks in woodland, heathland, or long grass, check [dog name] carefully around the ears, neck, armpits, and between the toes. If you find one, use a tick removal tool and twist gently - do not squeeze or pull straight off.

**Seasonal note:** Active March to October.

## 2. Oak Processionary Moth

**Key:** `oakmoth`
**Trigger:** Month 5-7 (May to July)
**Terrain:** Oak woodland, primarily SE England and London

**Title:** Oak moth season

**Alert body:**
Oak processionary moth caterpillars are active on oak trees this time of year - look out for white silken nests on trunks and branches. Keep [dog name] away from any nests and from the ground beneath affected trees, as the tiny hairs are hazardous even after falling from the nest. If [dog name] has been near any, rinse their mouth and eyes with clean water and contact your vet straight away.

**Seasonal note:** Active May to July, mainly SE England and London.

## 3. Lungworm

**Key:** `lungworm`
**Trigger:** Month 8-10 peak (year-round risk)
**Terrain:** Any damp ground, woodland, gardens

**Title:** Slugs and snails

**Alert body:**
[Dog name] can pick up lungworm from eating slugs or snails - worth actively discouraging that habit, especially on damp evening walks. If [dog name] is not already on a specific lungworm preventative (standard worming products do not cover it), it is worth a quick chat with your vet.

**Seasonal note:** Peak risk August to October, but present year-round.

---

## 4. Fungi and Mushrooms

**Key:** `fungi`
**Trigger:** Month 8-11 (August to November)
**Terrain:** Woodland, grassland, anywhere with decaying matter

**Title:** Autumn mushroom season

**Alert body:**
Autumn mushrooms are appearing in woodland and grassland across the UK. Keep [dog name] away from any mushrooms on the ground - some UK species are highly toxic and symptoms can take several hours to show. If you think [dog name] has eaten one, call your vet straight away and do not wait for signs.

**Seasonal note:** Active August to November.

---

## 5. Antifreeze

**Key:** `antifreeze`
**Trigger:** Month 11-3 (November to March)
**Terrain:** Urban and suburban - driveways, car parks, roadsides

**Title:** Antifreeze this winter

**Alert body:**
Antifreeze is used on driveways and car parks over winter and tastes sweet to dogs. Keep [dog name] away from puddles near parked cars and along roadsides on your walk. If you think [dog name] has licked any, get to a vet immediately - do not wait to see how they seem.

**Seasonal note:** Higher risk November to March.

---

## 6. Cattle

**Key:** `cattle`
**Trigger:** Month 3-6 elevated risk (year-round)
**Terrain:** Farmland, public footpaths through fields

**Title:** Cattle in fields

**Alert body:**
Cattle can be unpredictable around dogs, especially during calving season in spring. Keep [dog name] on a short lead when walking through fields with cattle nearby. If cattle start to approach, let [dog name] off the lead, do not run, and walk calmly to the nearest gate or field edge.

**Seasonal note:** Year-round presence - highest risk March to June during calving.
