## Context and guiding principle

Sniffout's audience is active UK dog owners who are motivated by their dog's safety and wellbeing. They are sceptical of advertising, trust recommendations from sources that feel knowledgeable, and they share content when it matches their values. The brand principle is non-negotiable: only recommend what you would genuinely recommend. One misplaced or irrelevant affiliate link does more damage than 10 well-placed ones. Every link must serve the reader before it serves revenue.

Phase 1 target: 0 to 1,000 active users. Revenue expectation at this stage should be modest, £50 to £300 per month at typical Phase 1 traffic volumes. The affiliate strategy must be set up correctly from the start so that it scales naturally as traffic grows without requiring a structural rebuild.

### Recurring commissions

UK pet insurance affiliate programmes pay acquisition commissions only -- a single CPA when the policy is first purchased. There are no confirmed renewal or recurring commission structures available through the affiliate networks covered. This differs from traditional insurance brokerage (which earns renewal income). For Phase 1 revenue planning, assume all insurance commissions are one-time.

Subscription dog food (Butternut Box, tails.com) similarly pays one-time CPAs on the first order -- there is no recurring commission on future deliveries. The strategic implication is that income from subscriptions requires a regular flow of new readers, not a compounding base of existing readers.

### Traffic required to generate £500/month

**Scenario A: Pet insurance focus (£20 CPA, 1% conversion, 20% click-through on relevant pages)**
- Required: 25 policy sales per month
- Clicks to insurance programme: 2,500
- Sessions to insurance-relevant content (20% CTR): 12,500 monthly sessions

- Required: 30 bookings per month
- Clicks to Rover: 750
- Sessions to relevant content: 3,000 monthly sessions (achievable at Phase 1 traffic)

- Income diversified across insurance, food, equipment, Rover
- Requires 15,000 to 30,000 monthly sessions to reach £500/month reliably

**Conclusion:** At Phase 1 traffic levels, affiliate income from Sniffout will be supplementary rather than material. ## Section 3 -- Placement Strategy

### Walk pages

Walk pages have two natural affiliate placement windows.

**1. The stats block (top of walk page)**

The off-lead status, terrain type, and difficulty information that appears in the stat pills creates an implied context. A walk tagged "On-lead only" with "livestock" in the area triggers an entirely different product consideration than an off-lead woodland walk. Do not place affiliate links in the stats block itself -- this area must stay clean and information-only. But the stats can inform which contextual block appears later.

**2. A single contextual recommendation block (after the description, before nearby walks)**

On a walk page, one affiliate placement is the maximum. It should be a small, clean block -- not a banner, not a product list. One product, one reason, one link. The block should be presented in a style consistent with the "For your dog" box (green tint, contextual label) rather than looking like an advertisement.

Recommended placement structure:
- Heading: "Recommended for this walk" or "Worth having"
- One product, one sentence of explanation, one text link (not a button, not an image)
- Clearly labelled with "(Ad)" at the start of the block

Content mapping by walk type:

| Walk type | Natural affiliate placement | Programme |
|---|---|---|
| On-lead park (e.g. Richmond Park) | Tick prevention awareness + VioVet link | VioVet via AWIN |
| Off-lead woodland / heathland | Tick treatment, harness recommendation | VioVet, Ruffwear |
| Coastal or river walk | Cooling water bottle, waterproof lead | Ruffwear, Zooplus |
| Summer walk (any) | Cooling vest or mat if temperature context is set | Ruffwear, Zooplus |
| Any walk | Pet insurance prompt (for first-time dog walkers exploring the site) | Petplan, Everypaw |

**What to avoid on walk pages:**
- More than one affiliate link per page
- Generic "you might also like" product rails
- Any product placement that is not directly relevant to the specific walk or walk type

---

### Hazard guide articles

Guide articles have the strongest affiliate fit of any page type on the site. A reader arriving on a tick safety article has demonstrated a specific interest that maps directly to products. These pages can carry more affiliate content than walk pages, but still with editorial discipline.

**Ticks article (sniffout.co.uk/guides/ticks-dogs-uk)**

This is the highest-priority affiliate article on the site.

Natural placements (2 to 3 maximum):
1. Tick removal tools section: link to O'Tom Tick Twister on Amazon Associates. One line: "The O'Tom Tick Twister is the tool recommended by most UK vets for safe tick removal." Link the product name only.
2. Tick prevention products section: link to a specific spot-on tick prevention product on VioVet. One line, one product, one link. "VioVet stocks a range of prescription and non-prescription tick treatments -- your vet can advise which is right for your dog."
3. Optional: pet insurance section (if the article discusses disease risks from tick bites such as Lyme disease). One link to Petplan or Everypaw framed around "if your dog picks up a tick-borne illness, comprehensive insurance will cover treatment."

The tick article is also the strongest candidate for a "Recommended kit" section -- a minimal list (3 items maximum) at the end of the article. Example: tick twister (Amazon), tick prevention spray or spot-on (VioVet), and a vet-approved tick-check reminder. This performs well on content sites and feels genuinely useful rather than commercial.

---

**Heatstroke and hot weather walking article**

Natural placements (2 maximum):
1. Cooling equipment: Ruffwear Swamp Cooler or a general cooling mat from Zooplus. Place in a "Before you go" or "Gear" section. "On very hot days, a cooling vest can significantly reduce your dog's temperature. The Ruffwear Swamp Cooler is worth considering for active dogs in warm weather."
2. Pet insurance: frame around emergency vet treatment costs if heat illness occurs. One link, contextual.

Do not place affiliate links in the immediate danger / symptom recognition sections. These sections must read as pure safety information without any commercial feel.

---

This is a sensitive topic. Alabama rot has no known cure and causes significant distress. Affiliate placement here is inappropriate. The article should be written as authoritative safety information only. No affiliate links. The trust value of this article (if it ranks well) is worth more than any commission it could generate.

---

**Blue-green algae article**

Similarly sensitive -- affected dogs can die within hours of ingestion. No affiliate links. This article serves brand credibility and SEO only.

---

**Cattle and dog walkers article**

Low affiliate fit -- no product directly prevents cattle incidents. Do not place affiliate links on this article. Clean, editorial only.

**Antifreeze article**

### How AllTrails handles affiliate links in editorial content

AllTrails uses a combination of sponsored partnerships (labelled) and editorial equipment recommendations (not always labelled). Their gear guides ("Best Dog Hiking Gear") function as editorial listicles with affiliated product links. They do not place affiliate links on individual trail pages. Neither the homepage nor the install page should carry affiliate links. These pages serve conversion (to app install) and brand trust. Any affiliate placement would dilute both. Keep them clean.

## Section 5 -- Content and Compliance

### ASA and CAP Code requirements

The ASA (Advertising Standards Authority) applies the CAP Code to all marketing communications in the UK, including affiliate content on blogs and editorial websites. Key requirements:

**The core rule:** Any communication where affiliate income might be earned must be "obviously identifiable as a marketing communication" before the consumer engages with it. This is Rule 2.1 of the CAP Code.

**What "obviously identifiable" requires:**
- The label must appear at the top of the article or section, before any affiliate links
- It must be prominent, not buried in a footer or visible only on scrolling
- It must use language that a general consumer understands -- not industry terms like "affiliate" which the ASA has ruled is not universally understood

**Labels the ASA considers acceptable:**
- "Ad"
- "Advertisement"
- "Sponsored"
- "(Ad)" placed at the start of a product recommendation

**Labels the ASA has ruled insufficient:**
- "May receive commission" -- implies possibility rather than confirming reality
- "*affiliate" -- the word "affiliate" is not considered clearly understood by general consumers
- Disclosure only in an About page or Privacy Policy -- this is not "before engagement"
- Disclosure at the bottom of an article -- the reader engaged with the content before seeing it

**Both the publisher and the advertiser (brand) are responsible for compliance.** If an AWIN-listed brand instructs a publisher to add a link that is not disclosed, both parties are potentially liable.

---

### ICO and cookie requirements

Affiliate tracking relies on cookies placed by the affiliate network (AWIN, Webgains, etc.) when a user clicks a link. These are classified as non-essential cookies under PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations).

**Current requirement:** Non-essential cookies require explicit opt-in consent before being set. A cookie consent banner that blocks affiliate tracking scripts by default until consent is given is legally required.

**Practical implementation:**
- Use a cookie consent management platform: Cookiebot, CookieYes, or Usercentrics are all ICO-compliant options for UK sites
- Configure the platform to block AWIN, Webgains, Amazon Associates, and other network scripts until the user provides consent
- List all affiliate networks and their cookie durations in the Cookie Policy
- Include in the Privacy Policy that referral data is shared with affiliate networks

**Forthcoming change:** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 received Royal Assent and includes language that may create a narrower consent-free pathway for purely statistical affiliate attribution tracking. However, this is not yet in force as of March 2026. Implementation guidance from the ICO is expected in mid-2026. Until then, the existing PECR requirement applies. Monitor this and update the cookie implementation when ICO guidance is published.

**ICO enforcement risk for a small publisher:** Low in practice at Phase 1 traffic levels. However, compliance costs are low (a cookie consent tool costs £0 to £10/month for a small site) and the reputational risk of appearing non-compliant in a category (dog safety) where trust is the core asset is not worth taking.

---

### Recommended disclosure wording

The following disclosure should appear at the top of any article or page that contains affiliate links. It should be placed before any affiliate link appears in the content, visually prominent (not styled as grey footer text), and not hidden by expanding "read more" on mobile.

**In-article disclosure (short form, for placement at the top of guide articles):**

> This article contains affiliate links, marked with (Ad) where they appear. If you buy through these links, Sniffout earns a small commission at no extra cost to you. We only link to products we would genuinely recommend.

This wording is compliant because:
- It confirms commission is earned (not "may be earned")
- It uses clear consumer language
- It states the user pays no extra cost
- It includes an editorial integrity statement consistent with the brand principle
- It is short enough to be read and not scrolled past

**Individual in-content label:** Each specific affiliate link or product block should also be labelled "(Ad)" immediately before the product name or link, consistent with ASA requirements for partial-article affiliate content.

---

### How leading UK pet content sites handle disclosure

The dominant practice among established UK pet content sites (The Kennel Club blog, Dogs Monthly, Your Dog Magazine digital, Canine Cottages) is to place a short disclosure statement at the top of any article with commercial content, followed by "(Ad)" or "Affiliate link" labels on specific recommendations. This is the pattern Sniffout should follow.

The sites that handle it best do so by making the disclosure feel like part of the editorial voice: "We want to be upfront: this article contains affiliate links" is more trusted than "Disclosure: this post may contain affiliate links." The first is a statement of values; the second sounds like legal boilerplate.

## Section 6 -- What to Avoid

### Products and programmes that damage trust

**Generic pet product aggregators:** Programmes from large generic comparison sites or product aggregators that pay to be placed in content feel incongruous on a brand-specific, curated site. If a link looks like it could have been placed by anyone, it probably should not be placed by Sniffout.

**Supplements with unsupported health claims:** CBD for dogs, "calming" supplements with no veterinary evidence base, joint supplements from brands not reviewed by independent parties. These categories are popular in affiliate content and poorly regulated. A link to an unproven supplement on a dog safety-focused site is a credibility liability.

**Pet food brands with poor nutritional track records:** Not all pet food brands with affiliate programmes should be promoted. If a brand has faced regulatory action, poor independent reviews, or questionable ingredient sourcing, do not link to it regardless of commission rate.

**Animigo UK:** High commission rate (15 to 25%) is notable but Animigo's products sit in a loosely regulated supplement category. Only place these links in highly specific, relevant contexts (if at all) and ensure any claims about the products are verifiable. The commission rate alone is not a reason to promote.

---

### Networks and programmes with poor reputations or unreliable payments

**PetDrugs Online (1% commission):** The effective EPC from this programme is too low to be worth the implementation overhead. Prioritise VioVet instead.

**Unknown or unverified direct programmes:** Smaller brands that run their own affiliate programmes outside a recognised network may have unreliable tracking, slow validation, or non-payment issues. Before joining any direct programme (not on AWIN, Webgains, or TradeDoubler), research payment history and tracking reliability. The lack of network oversight means disputes are harder to resolve.

**Amazon Associates 24-hour cookie:** Not a trust issue but a structural limitation. The 24-hour cookie makes Amazon Associates ineffective for any research-oriented content where users take more than a day to purchase. Use it only for intent-dense, purchase-ready content (tick tool recommendation after a clear "how to remove a tick" section).

---

### Over-monetisation patterns

**Walk page product rails:** A walk page with 3 to 5 product links looks like a monetisation page, not a walk guide. This damages both user trust and SEO. Google's quality assessments now heavily penalise pages where the primary purpose appears to be affiliate income rather than user benefit. Walk pages must remain clean. One contextual link maximum.

**Affiliate links in safety-critical sections:** Never place an affiliate link in a section that describes emergency symptoms, immediate action steps, or life-threatening situations. Alabama rot, blue-green algae, heatstroke symptoms -- these sections must read as pure, uninterested safety information. Affiliate links here are both ethically wrong and legally risky (they could be construed as steering readers toward commercial rather than safe options).

**Commission-chasing over editorial fit:** A commission rate of 25% from Animigo is not a reason to write a supplement article. Write articles the audience needs and find affiliate fits within them -- not the reverse. Sites that chase high-commission categories with thin content are consistently penalised by Google's Helpful Content system, which was strengthened in the March 2026 core update.

**Affiliate-only pages:** Do not create pages whose sole purpose is to list affiliate links (e.g. "Best dog cooling vests 2026" as a thin affiliate round-up). These pages have poor SEO performance and damage the brand. Any editorial content must stand on its own merit as genuinely useful, with affiliate links as an optional supplement.

---

### Programmes specifically problematic for a dog safety brand

**Any programme offering unverified "natural" tick prevention alternatives:** Products marketed as natural tick repellents (essential oil sprays, herbal collars) often have no clinical efficacy evidence and some have toxicity risks for dogs. Linking to these on a tick safety article would directly contradict the editorial purpose.

**Pet travel insurance (general):** Not to be confused with standard pet insurance. Some travel pet insurance programmes have high commissions but significant exclusion clauses that make them poor value for UK dog walkers. If pet insurance is promoted, stick to established, well-rated UK insurers (Petplan, Everypaw, Agria) rather than chasing commission from newer or less established brands.

### 4 -- Recommended disclosure wording

Place the following at the top of any article or page containing affiliate links. Style it consistently with body text -- readable, not hidden, not greyed out. Do not place it at the bottom of the page.

> This article contains affiliate links, marked with (Ad) where they appear. If you buy through these links, Sniffout earns a small commission at no extra cost to you. We only link to products we would genuinely recommend to a fellow dog owner.

Additionally, label each individual affiliate product or recommendation block with "(Ad)" immediately before the product name or link.
